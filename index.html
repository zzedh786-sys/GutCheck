GutCheck
How it works

Scan a packaged food

Point your camera at a barcode. GutCheck looks the product up on Open Food Facts and checks its ingredients against a low FODMAP traffic-light guide — the same one used in the Gutwise tracker.

Type a barcode instead

Your camera video stays on your device — only the barcode number is sent, to Open Food Facts, to look the product up.

How this works, and its limits

GutCheck reads a barcode with your camera, then asks Open Food Facts — a free, crowd-sourced product database — for that product's ingredients list. It runs that text through the same FODMAP-matching engine as the Gutwise tracker, using the traffic-light system: green (low FODMAP), amber (moderate, mind the serve size) and red (high FODMAP).

  • Coverage varies. Open Food Facts is strongest for UK and EU packaged foods and weaker for smaller, regional or freshly-launched products. A "not found" result doesn't mean a product is safe or unsafe — just that it isn't in the database yet.
  • It's reading Open Food Facts' ingredient text, not the Monash database. The FODMAP traffic-light ratings come from a curated list based on published Monash University research, not Monash's own licensed database or app.
  • Always check the pack if a result looks wrong, incomplete, or the product has changed its recipe since it was catalogued.
  • Camera scanning works in most Chrome, Edge and Android browsers, and in Safari/iOS via a fallback library. If your camera won't start, use "Type a barcode instead".

This tool is for tracking and information, and isn't medical advice.
